    Why Choose BVL Plumbing for Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Hagerstown, MD?

    Our plumbers size an ejector by lift, run and fixture count, not by what is on the truck. A pump that has to push waste 9 feet up and 40 feet across needs a different curve than one lifting 6 feet straight to a drain right overhead. The curve decides it. Matching it means fewer starts and a longer motor life.

    Ejector Pump Alarms and Backup Options in Hagerstown

    A high water alarm should be on any ejector basin, since a pump failure can lead to a sewage backup quickly. Audible alarms on their own power or Wi-Fi alarms that text you give warning while the pit is still filling, not after waste reaches the floor. Low-lying waterfront areas also face tidal flooding, which can back up drains and affect septic systems near the shore. Battery backup is an option.

    Ejector Pump Installation Cost Comparison in Hagerstown, MD

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin costs the least, often done in a few hours. A full new system with basin, floor excavation, roof vent, electrical and alarm costs more, but it is usually the only way to add a below-sewer bath besides a macerator. Ask for both numbers.

    Zoeller Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Hagerstown

    Zoeller's sewage line covers home and heavier-duty use, in cast iron with tethered or vertical float switches. We pick the model by horsepower and pump curve for your lift, and a typical basement bathroom lands on a four-tenths or half horsepower unit with two-inch solids handling. Bigger lifts need more.

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Hagerstown

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps seem similar at first but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. Never mix the two.

Venting still applies.
