    Cracked Toilet Replacement in Frederick

    Porcelain cracks below the waterline are not safely repairable, and epoxy fixes on a tank under constant water pressure rarely last more than a few months. You should replace it. We pull the cracked toilet and check the floor for water damage before setting the new one, since a slow crack leak often soaks the subfloor first.

    Replacing Pressure-Assisted Toilets in Frederick, MD

    Pressure-assisted toilets use a sealed vessel in the tank that stores air under line pressure and pushes water through the bowl hard, which helps in houses with long, low-slope drain runs. They are noticeably louder than gravity toilets. Replacement means matching the vessel brand for parts later, and we check that house pressure is in the range the unit needs.

    Rental and Multi-Unit Toilet Replacement in Frederick, MD

    Landlords with several units do better standardizing on one toilet model with common parts, because the next flapper or fill valve is then the same part in every unit. Multiple toilets can be swapped in a single trip. Tenants keep a working bathroom. We coordinate entry directly with them, so you do not have to be there.

    Standard Toilet Replacement in Frederick

    Two-piece toilets are what most houses have, and after twenty or thirty years the tank bolts corrode, the porcelain gets crazed and the flush never quite clears the bowl. That is when we swap them. We pull the old toilet, scrape the flange and check it for cracks, then set the new one on a fresh wax ring with new closet bolts.

    Water-Saving Toilet Upgrades in Frederick, MD

    Older toilets are one of the largest indoor water users in a house, and replacing a 3.5 gallon model with a 1.28 gallon one cuts toilet water use by more than half. Dual-flush models go lower for liquids. Our recommendations lean on high MaP scores so the savings do not come with double flushing.

    Emergency Toilet Replacement in Frederick

    A toilet that cracked overnight and is still seeping into the floor cannot wait for next week, so we bring a standard round and elongated model on emergency calls. First, close the valve behind the toilet. Most emergency swaps are done the same day, and we check the subfloor for soaked plywood before setting it.

    High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Frederick, MD

    Toilets made before 1994 often use 3.5 gallons a flush, and some from the 1970s use five or more. A WaterSense model uses 1.28 gallons or less, and the better ones still clear the bowl in one try because the trapway and flush valve are larger than they used to be. Savings show up fast. Some water providers offer a rebate, so ask before you buy.
