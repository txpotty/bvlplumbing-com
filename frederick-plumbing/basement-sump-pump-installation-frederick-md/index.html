    No pit yet? First we locate the low point where water gathers, saw cut the slab, dig roughly 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a liner surrounded by washed gravel so water can enter while fine soil stays out. We pour new concrete flush with the floor.

    Zoeller's M53 Mighty-Mate is the cast iron pump we install more than any other. It runs at 3/10 horsepower on a vertical float switch, and it moves about 2,580 gallons an hour at 5 feet of lift, enough for a lot of Frederick basements. Replacement switches are on every supply shelf.

    Quiet Submersible Pumps for Finished Basements in Frederick

    Finished basements call for a quiet pump and a sealed lid. A submersible with a gasketed cover keeps the noise down, and we route the discharge behind framing or through a closet so the pipe does not run across a wall you just painted in your Frederick home. We patch the slab flush.

    Pedestal Sump Pumps in Frederick

    Older homes often have narrow pits that will not fit a submersible. A pedestal pump fits where nothing else will, and we install it with rigid discharge, a quality check valve, and a float rod set to your pit depth so it starts before water reaches the top of the liner.     Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Frederick

    Pedestal pumps put the motor on a column above the water. That keeps it dry and easy to reach, which is why they suit narrow pits under 14 inches across, and why they often outlast cheap submersibles in Frederick homes. They are louder, though.
