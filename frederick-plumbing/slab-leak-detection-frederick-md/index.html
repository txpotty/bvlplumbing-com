    Hot Water Slab Leak Detection in Frederick

    The hot side fails more than the cold. Heat speeds up corrosion, and the pipe expands and contracts every time someone showers, rubbing against concrete and gravel until it wears through. Warm floors and a water heater that runs nonstop are the signs. Energy bills often rise.

    Pressure Testing and Isolation in Frederick

    Pressure isolation is always the first step. Our tech closes the main, caps or valves off the hot and cold sides, and puts a gauge on each, then watches which side loses pressure over a set time. That tells us where to look. It also rules out a toilet or faucet leak masquerading as a slab leak in your Frederick, MD home.

    Residential Slab Leak Detection in Frederick

    Single-family homes and townhouses across Frederick all go through the same testing. Meter test first. We isolate, trace and listen, then confirm with a thermal scan or tracer gas, then explain what we found and give you written repair prices.

    Thermal Scans on Large Floors in Frederick, MD

    Big open floor plans hide leaks well. A thermal camera scans an entire living area in a few minutes, showing temperature differences of a degree or less across tile or concrete, so we can rule out half the house before setting up the microphone. It saves an hour on large homes across Frederick, MD.

    Direct Access Slab Leak Repair in Frederick, MD

    One leak on healthy pipe is a good spot-repair candidate. We cut a small opening at the marked spot, dig down to the pipe, replace the bad section and hold a pressure test. Then we backfill and patch the concrete level, ready for flooring. Accurate marks keep that hole small.

    Cold Water Slab Leak Detection in Frederick, MD

    Cold-side leaks leave no warm spot. They are harder to see. They usually show up as a running meter, damp carpet, a musty smell or water seeping at baseboards, which is why we isolate the cold line, trace its route and listen along it with acoustic gear.

    Slab Leak Reroutes in Frederick

    A reroute abandons the leaking line under the floor. New pipe runs through the walls or attic instead, which avoids breaking concrete and often costs less than opening a finished floor. We usually use PEX because it handles heat and resists corrosion. Tile and hardwood floors make this the favorite.

    Post-Tension Slab Leak Detection in Frederick

    Many newer homes sit on post-tension slabs. These slabs have steel tendons stretched under tens of thousands of pounds of force, and cutting one by mistake is dangerous and can damage the foundation. We scan for tendons with ground-penetrating radar or a rebar locator before opening anything, or recommend a reroute that never touches the concrete.
