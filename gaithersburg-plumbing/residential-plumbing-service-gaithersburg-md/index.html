    Frequently Asked Questions About Residential Plumbing Service in Gaithersburg

    Most minor repairs run $150 to $450. Replacing a water heater runs $1,400 to $3,000, depending on size and whether it is gas or electric. The flat price is yours to approve first.

    Soft water and normal pressure prevent a lot of what we see. Keep grease out of the drains and test pressure once a year, since anything over 80 psi wears out supply hoses and fixtures faster than people expect. An annual check finds small issues early.

    Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. Soft water has had most of those minerals removed, usually by an ion-exchange softener, and the difference shows up as scale in water heaters, white crust on faucets, spotty dishes and soap that will not lather well. Soft water is easier on fixtures.

    Every repair comes with a written parts and labor warranty. New fixtures and water heaters also carry manufacturer coverage, and we register it for you, which matters because some water heater makers shorten the tank warranty if the unit is never registered. Save the serial number.

    Watch for drips that will not stop, slow drains, banging or whistling pipes, a sudden drop in pressure, discolored water and wet spots with no obvious source. Any one of these points to a problem behind the wall or under the floor. Do not ignore them.

    Insulate any pipe in an unheated space, like a crawlspace, attic or garage wall. Unhook hoses from the spigots and open sink cabinets on exterior walls so warm air reaches the pipes, and on the coldest nights let a faucet drip at a slow, steady pace. If you will be away, shut off the main. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated crawlspaces and garages are the usual trouble spots when a deep cold snap settles in.

    Some jobs are fine to DIY. Swapping a flapper or clearing a sink trap is well within reach, but gas lines, water heaters and anything inside a wall can turn into a flood, a fire risk or a failed inspection if the work is off. For those, hire a licensed plumber.

    Two leaks in a year is the tipping point for most homes. Polybutylene or galvanized supply pipe pushes it toward a repipe, too, since patching one spot on a failing system just moves the next leak a few feet down the line. We price both side by side.
