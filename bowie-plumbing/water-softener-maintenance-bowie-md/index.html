    Service for Most Softener Brands in Bowie, MD

    Control Valve and Injector Cleaning in Bowie, MD

    Brine draw depends on a venturi injector, a tiny nozzle and throat that pulls brine out of the salt tank as water rushes past. A few specks of grit can stop it cold. We pull the injector and screen, clean them, and replace the seals and spacers on valves like the Fleck 5600 and Clack WS1 if they are worn.

    Full-System Softener Inspections in Bowie

    An inspection covers more than the two tanks. The inlet and outlet piping, bypass, drain line and brine overflow all get traced and checked. Green crust on copper fittings, a slow leak at the bypass or a drain line jammed down into a standpipe all go on the written report.

    Bypass Valve and Drain Line Repairs in Bowie, MD

    A bypass valve that does not seat fully lets raw water blend into the softened supply, and the hardness test is usually the first clue. The bypass gets rebuilt or swapped out and check the drain line for kinks. Code requires an air gap at the drain.

    Yearly Maintenance Plans in Bowie, MD

    Plans take the remembering off your plate. We call ahead of each yearly visit, and a valve failure between visits moves you to the front of the line. Repairs found during a plan visit are billed at a reduced rate.

    Commercial Softener Service in Bowie

    Commercial units work harder. Restaurants and laundromats can burn through a softener's capacity in a single day, so a twin-tank system keeps one tank online while the other recharges. We set a service schedule around your hours so the water never goes hard during a rush.

    Yearly Water Softener Tune-Ups in Bowie

    A tune-up starts with two hardness readings and ends with a full manual regeneration that we watch from start to finish. Between those, the salt level, brine well and float all get a look. It takes about an hour on most homes.

    Resin Bed Checks and Regeneration Settings in Bowie

    Resin does the actual softening. The beads swap sodium for calcium and magnesium, and once they are loaded up they need brine to recharge. We compare the softened hardness against the gallons used since the last regeneration to tell whether the bed is still at full strength.
