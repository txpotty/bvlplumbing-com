    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Ellicott City

    Spot repairs on an accessible line usually run $350 to $1,500. The deeper the pipe, the higher the price. A trenched meter-to-house replacement is typically $1,800 to $5,500, and trenchless replacement often lands between $3,000 and $7,000 because of the equipment involved. The written price comes before we start.

    We work on copper, PEX, HDPE poly, PVC and old galvanized steel. We also run into gray polybutylene now and then. For new work in Ellicott City, Type K copper and burial-rated PEX are our usual picks, with HDPE for pulled lines. The trucks carry transition fittings for every one of them.

    We dig a pit at each end of the old line. A cable goes through the old pipe. A bursting head on the cable splits the old line outward, and new HDPE pipe follows right behind it into the same space. It only works when the old pipe is a fairly straight run, and most home service lines qualify.

    Yes, we stand behind it. If it leaks, we come back. The workmanship warranty covers our labor on the repaired section, and the manufacturer covers the new pipe and fittings for their own stated term. All of it is listed on the service agreement you sign before we start.

    Keep trees and shrubs with big roots well away from the line. Mark the pipe route somewhere. Disconnecting garden hoses and shutting off outdoor spigots before the first hard freeze prevents many burst-pipe calls each winter. A pressure gauge on a hose bib tells you if the regulator is failing, and anything over 80 psi is worth fixing before it starts wearing on the line. On galvanized lines, budgeting for a new line is cheaper than an emergency.

    Watch the bill first. A sudden jump with no change in use is the classic clue. Other signs include low pressure everywhere, soggy ground in dry weather, discolored water and a hissing sound near the meter. On a slab home, a warm spot on the floor can mean the leak is under the house.

    Type K copper, burial-rated PEX or HDPE poly. All three work well. Copper resists punctures and lasts decades, PEX flexes with the soil and HDPE is what we use for trenchless pulls. We recommend one after looking at your soil, line length and budget.
