    What Goes Into a Sewer Lateral Replacement in Waldorf, MD

    Cured-in-Place Sewer Lining in Waldorf

    Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. After cutting roots and jetting the line, we pull in a resin-filled liner and inflate it until it cures against the old pipe wall. Collapsed pipe will not work. It is a strong choice under slabs and finished rooms. We say plainly whether your line qualifies.

    Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Waldorf

    Cast iron rots from the inside out. Sewer gas and moisture eat away the bottom of the pipe first, so the camera often shows a channel worn through along the invert even when the top still looks solid. Scale snags paper and grease. We swap the outdoor section for plastic pipe. For cast iron under a slab, we talk through lining versus rerouting.

    Pipe Bursting Under Driveways in Waldorf, MD

    Concrete is expensive to replace. Bursting lets us run new HDPE under a driveway, patio or mature tree by pulling it through the path of the old pipe from a launch pit to a receiving pit. The HDPE is butt-fused on site into one long piece. Most bursts finish in a day. You end up patching two small pits rather than a whole slab.

    Sewer Camera Inspection in Waldorf

    No replacement quote without video. We run a self-leveling color camera from the cleanout to the main and record every joint, crack, root mass and low spot along the way, with footage you keep. A sonde in the camera head lets us mark the depth on the lawn. That one step decides between a spot repair and a full run.

    Sewer Permits and Code in Waldorf, MD

    Sewer replacement nearly always needs a permit. We file it, schedule the inspection and meet the inspector on site, so you never have to take a morning off to stand in the yard. Code covers pipe size, fall, bedding and cleanout placement. Unpermitted sewer work can show up on a home inspection when you sell.

    Emergency Sewer Replacement in Waldorf, MD

If it has collapsed, we start permit and locate requests that day. BVL Plumbing can often run a temporary bypass. That keeps one toilet and the kitchen sink usable while the dig gets scheduled.
