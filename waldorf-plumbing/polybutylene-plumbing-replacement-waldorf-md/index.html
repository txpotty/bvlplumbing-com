    How We Replace Polybutylene in Waldorf, MD

    Full Home Repiping with PEX in Waldorf

    Most poly replacements we do use PEX-A, a flexible tubing that runs long distances with few fittings. On a slab home, we route the new lines overhead through the attic and down interior walls, which keeps every supply line out of the concrete. PEX handles a freeze better than copper. We use expansion fittings that keep the full inside diameter open, so pressure at the far shower stays strong.

    Yard Service Line Replacement in Waldorf, MD

    Many poly-era homes also have a blue or black polybutylene service line running from the water meter to the house. These buried lines can split under the yard, raising the water bill and soaking the soil. Utilities get marked first. After locating, we trench, lay new PEX or copper and tie it into a new main shutoff at the house.

    Partial Polybutylene Replacement in Waldorf, MD

    Full replacement is the right long-term fix, but it is not always possible right away. Sometimes it has to wait. Replacing the riskiest parts first, near the heater and in the attic, lowers the odds of a burst while you plan the rest. You will know exactly what remains.

    Whole-Home PEX Replacement in Waldorf, MD

    PEX-A is the most common replacement for polybutylene because it is flexible, scale resistant and easy to route. We use Uponor PEX-A with expansion fittings that do not restrict flow. New lines run from the main shutoff to every fixture, water heater and appliance, and most homes are completed in two to four working days with water back on each night.

    Polybutylene Replacement Costs in Waldorf, MD

    Replacing polybutylene with PEX in an average home often costs $5,000 to $12,000, depending on size and bathrooms. Copper runs higher. A yard service line typically adds $1,500 to $4,000, and that is still usually less than repairing damage from one burst.

    Copper Replacement for Polybutylene in Waldorf, MD

    Some homeowners in Waldorf, MD want copper when they take out polybutylene. That works fine. Our copper replacements use thicker Type L pipe with lead-free soldered or pressed joints, not the thinner Type M. Copper costs more and takes longer, since each turn needs a fitting, but it tolerates heat and sunlight well and has a long track record.

    Full Home Polybutylene Repiping in Waldorf

    A full replacement takes every poly supply line out of service, not just the sections that have failed. Partial jobs leave risk behind. We run new lines from the main shutoff to every sink, toilet, tub, washer, ice maker and water heater, add new quarter-turn stops and test the whole system before patching.

    Polybutylene to PEX Transitions in Waldorf, MD

    Sometimes the yard service line coming into the house is not polybutylene, or it was replaced already. In that case, we connect the new PEX system to the existing service at the main shutoff with an approved transition fitting, which avoids digging up the yard. The service line gets checked. Blue or black poly service pipe should come out as well.
