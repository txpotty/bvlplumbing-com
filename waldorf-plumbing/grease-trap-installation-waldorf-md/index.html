    Grease Trap Sizing and Placement in Waldorf, MD

    Sizing starts with fixture drain rates and ends with where the trap can physically go. Long runs to the trap let grease cool and coat the line, so we keep the trap as close to the fixtures as we can. Closer is better. We also check that a pump truck can reach an outdoor tank with about 100 feet of hose, since a tank the hauler cannot reach costs you more on every service.

    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Installation in Waldorf

    Outdoor gravity interceptors run from 750 gallons to well past 2,000, and setting one means an excavator on site and a traffic-rated lid if it sits in the drive lane. Before anything ships, we check that the tank and covers carry an H-20 traffic rating where trucks will roll over them. Most go in over two to four days. The inlet invert has to sit a couple of inches above the outlet, and we shoot both with a laser level before backfill.

    Restaurant Grease Trap Compliance in Waldorf, MD

    Most sewer authorities run a FOG program with an inspector who opens your trap and checks your pump-out manifests against the schedule they set. A failed inspection can bring a fine or an order to upgrade. We install to pass. We add a sampling port if they require it and size to the authority's own formula.

    Grease Trap Replacement and Upgrades in Waldorf

    Plastic and steel traps both wear out, just in different ways. Steel rusts at the seams and the baffle welds. Plastic cracks. When we pull an old trap we cap the lines, haul it out, set the new unit on the same footprint where possible and reconnect with no-hub couplings so the next swap goes faster.

    Replacement and Upgrade Services in Waldorf, MD

    Steel traps from decades back rust through at the baffle seams, and by then the outlet is usually passing grease straight to the sewer. A camera down the outlet line tells us how much damage the old unit already did. Sometimes the line needs jetting too. If your seats or menu grew since opening, the swap is the time to step up to a bigger GPM rating.

    Gravity Grease Trap Installation in Waldorf

    A gravity interceptor works on time, not machinery. The tank holds water for roughly half an hour, which gives grease time to rise and food solids time to drop. We size by fixture count and retention time, then match the tank to what the local pretreatment office for your building will accept. No moving parts to fail.

    Grease Trap Installation Cost in Waldorf, MD

    Price follows size and location more than brand. A small indoor trap set on an open floor near the sink is the cheapest version of this job. Outdoor tanks with digging and a permit sit at the top of the range. We quote after a site visit.

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Waldorf

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow. We pipe the air intake the way the manufacturer and the code both require. Get that wrong and the trap siphons. A trap that sits out of level loses capacity, so we shim it flat before connecting anything.

    Expect roughly $1,500 to $4,000 for a small indoor trap and $7,000 to $20,000 or more for an outdoor gravity interceptor. A floor-recessed trap costs more than one set on the floor.     From first call to final inspection, most indoor jobs take one to two weeks, and most of that is waiting on the permit and the part. Our time on site is usually one day. Outdoor jobs run longer. Allow a month or more for an outdoor tank, counting plan review and delivery.

    Half a day for most indoor units. We set it, connect it, add the flow control and test it with water. Gravity interceptors take two to five days on site, plus time for the inspector to see the tank before backfill and again at final. Permits add lead time.
