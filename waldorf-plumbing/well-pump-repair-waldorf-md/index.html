    Well Pump Problems We Fix in Waldorf, MD

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Waldorf

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Most switches fail from pitted contacts, a plugged sensing tube or insects in the housing. After swapping the switch and clearing the tube, we set cut-in and cut-out to match the tank, usually 40 and 60 psi. One of the cheaper well repairs.

    Jet Pump Repair in Waldorf, MD

    Shallow wells and booster systems often use jet pumps. Common problems are loss of prime, a leaking shaft seal and a worn impeller. Our crew in Waldorf restores prime, replaces worn parts and checks the suction line for leaks that let air in. That matters most where water levels rise and fall through the year, since a dropping level can starve the pump and cost it prime.

    Above-Ground Jet Pump Repair in Waldorf

    Lost prime is the number one jet pump complaint. These pumps can also leak at the shaft seal or wear out an impeller. The pump gets reprimed and resealed, and the foot valve and suction line get checked, because a single air leak will undo the work. Everything is above ground.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Waldorf

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. The usual signs are no water at all, weak pressure or a hum with no flow. We test the motor from the surface first. If it has to come up, a pump hoist lifts the pipe safely, and we replace the burned motor, worn impellers or a bad check valve before setting it back.

    Jet Pump System Repair in Waldorf, MD

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Most failures start with an air leak somewhere in the suction line. BVL Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and confirms it builds pressure. A jet pump that runs nonstop without reaching pressure has almost always lost prime or sucked air through a cracked fitting.

    Grundfos Well Pump Repair in Waldorf

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Most Grundfos calls involve a sensor or a drive fault. BVL Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a Waldorf, MD well. Usually the electronics are fine.

    Control Box and Electrical Repair in Waldorf, MD

    Capacitors, relays and corroded terminals inside the control box cause many pump failures. Breaker trips usually come first. With the power locked out, we test and replace the failed parts and tighten every terminal. Wiring meets Maryland code before we restart the pump.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Waldorf, MD

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes the pump short cycle, turning on and off every few seconds. That rapid cycling burns out pumps early. The air charge gets checked and the bladder inspected, then the tank is recharged or replaced so the pump runs a full minute per cycle. Steadier pressure follows.
