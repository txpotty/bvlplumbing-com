    Gas Leak Detection in Silver Spring

    Some leaks announce themselves with a hiss you can hear across the room, and others lose a few cubic feet a day and only show up on the bill. The slow ones are where the gauge earns its keep.     Permitting and Code Compliance in Silver Spring, MD

    Most jurisdictions want a permit for new gas pipe or a moved line, though a straight connector swap often does not need one. We tell you up front which way your job falls.     Gas Line Repair & Replacement in Silver Spring, MD

    Black iron pipe with threaded fittings is still the most common interior gas pipe, and the leak is nearly always at a thread, not in the middle of a length. We take the joint apart, clean the threads and remake it with fresh gas-rated sealant. Cracked fittings get replaced. Where a length has rusted badly, usually near a damp crawlspace vent, we cut back to sound pipe and splice in new. Every remade joint gets a detector check and a gauge test.

    Gas Line Replacement and Upgrades in Silver Spring, MD

    Adding a gas range or a tankless heater can overload pipe that was sized years ago for just a furnace and a water heater. Sizing starts with the total BTU load and the longest run from the meter. A big tankless unit may also need a larger meter from the utility.

    Commercial Gas Leak Services in Silver Spring

    Restaurant kitchens and small shops run more appliances on bigger pipe, often 1-1/4 inch or larger, fed from a meter with its own regulator.     Propane Gas Leak Repair in Silver Spring, MD

    Propane behaves differently from natural gas because it is heavier than air, so a leak settles into basements and crawlspaces instead of rising. We check both regulators, the one at the tank and the one at the house. Low spots get sniffed first. Your propane supplier owns the tank in most cases, so we coordinate with them for anything on the tank side of the first regulator. Rural properties with buried tanks get a line test too.

    Appliance Gas Line Repair in Silver Spring, MD

    Most appliance leaks show up at the flex connector or the shutoff valve behind the appliance, usually right at a flare nut where the two meet. Every time somebody slides the stove out to mop, the connector takes another bend. Valves weep at the stem. The fix is usually a new coated connector and a new ball valve, plus a drip leg if the appliance lacks one. Each joint gets a detector pass and a bubble test before the pilot is relit.
