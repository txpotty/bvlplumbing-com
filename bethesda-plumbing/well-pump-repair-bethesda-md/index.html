    Pressure Switch Replacement in Bethesda

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Most switches fail from pitted contacts, a plugged sensing tube or insects in the housing.     Pressure Tank Repair in Bethesda, MD

    A waterlogged pressure tank makes the pump short cycle, turning on and off every few seconds. Fast cycling kills pumps young. The air charge gets checked and the bladder inspected, then the tank is recharged or replaced so the pump runs a full minute per cycle. Steadier pressure follows.

    Control Box & Electrical Repair in Bethesda, MD

    Capacitors, relays and corroded terminals inside the control box cause many pump failures. A tripped breaker is often the first sign. With the power locked out, we test and replace the failed parts and tighten every terminal. Wiring meets Maryland code before we restart the pump.

    Control Box and Surge Repair in Bethesda, MD

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. Nor'easters, tropical storm remnants and summer downpours can overwhelm storm drains across Maryland and push water toward basements. Surges from lightning are the usual cause of control box failures. After testing the capacitor, relay and overload, we replace the bad part and suggest a surge arrestor if the box lacks one.     Low Water Pressure Repair in Bethesda

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. Common causes include a plugged sediment filter, a low switch setting or a yard line leak. In some cases the pump is fine and the well just cannot keep up with demand. We test each one.

    Well Pump Diagnostic Services in Bethesda, MD

    Voltage, amperage, pressure and flow rate tell us most of what is happening down the well without pulling a single length of pipe. A weak motor draws high amps. Low flow with normal amp draw points to a plugged intake or a falling water level, which is a different repair. Testing first saves a pull.
