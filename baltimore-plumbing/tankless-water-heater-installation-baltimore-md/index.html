    What Goes Into a Tankless Conversion in Baltimore, MD

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Baltimore, MD

    Switching from a tank is more than a swap. A 40-gallon tank burns about 40,000 BTU, but a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the meter line nearly always gets bigger. The vent changes as well. Both are on the estimate.

    Cost-Effective Tankless Conversions in Baltimore, MD

    A tankless conversion in Baltimore, MD costs more than a tank swap, mostly because of the gas line upgrade, the new venting and the unit price itself. The savings come back over time. With no standby loss and a lifespan that can reach 20 years, many homeowners see the difference on the gas bill within the first few winters. Your quote shows the math.

    Rinnai Tankless Water Heater Installation in Baltimore, MD

    Rinnai has been making tankless heaters for decades, and parts are easy to get. Their condensing and non-condensing lines both fit different homes, depending on the vent route and the budget you are working with. Non-condensing units vent in stainless. Condensing units vent in PVC and drain condensate.

    Residential Tankless System Upgrades in Baltimore

    Upgrading from a tank in Baltimore starts with a look at what you have now: tank size, vent type, gas line size and where the unit sits. In some homes the old flue gets capped and the new unit vents out a side wall in PVC, which is simpler and opens the old chase. Some homes need more vent pipe. We spell that out on the quote.

    Gas Tankless Water Heater Installation in Baltimore

    Gas tankless units in Baltimore are the common pick for whole-house hot water because a single 199,000 BTU burner can feed two showers and a sink at the same time without losing temperature. Gas is the catch. In most houses, the line from the meter has to be upsized, often from half-inch to three-quarter inch or bigger. We size it and pressure test it before the unit is lit.

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Baltimore

    Conversions in Baltimore tend to run a full workday. We drain and remove the tank, upsize and test the gas, run a new vent through a wall or roof, then mount, plumb and fire the unit with service valves. You shower that night. The inspector comes after.

    Propane Tankless Water Heater Installation in Baltimore

    Propane homes in Baltimore are good candidates for tankless, using a model built or converted for propane. Propane runs at a higher pressure than natural gas, so the regulator, gas line sizing and the unit's gas valve all have to match, and the propane supplier may need to change the regulator at the tank. Tank size matters too.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Baltimore, MD

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Baltimore, MD homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. A whole-home electric unit draws heavy amperage. Some models need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, more than a 100-amp panel can usually spare alongside the range and AC. We check the panel first.
