    What Goes Into a Well Tank Swap in Baltimore, MD

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Baltimore, MD

    Switch contacts pit and burn over time. Worn contacts can fail to open or close. When we swap a tank we look at the contacts and the sensing port underneath, and we usually recommend replacing a switch older than the tank it served. Cheap part.

    Constant Pressure System Conversion in Baltimore, MD

    Constant pressure systems use a variable speed drive that adjusts pump speed to demand. You get even pressure across fixtures. These systems can run on a smaller tank, though the drive and wiring add to the price. We explain the trade-off.

    Emergency Pressure Tank Replacement in Baltimore, MD

    If the pump is running nonstop or the taps are dry, call (443) 719-1650. A ruptured tank or a stuck switch can burn out a pump, and we work to get you running before the pump is damaged. Turn off the breaker first.

    Residential Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Baltimore

    Most homes on a well have a tank between 20 and 86 gallons sitting near the pressure switch. We shut off power to the pump first. Then we drain the system, unthread the old tank from the tank tee, set the pre-charge on the new one, and plumb it back in with fresh thread sealant and new fittings. Usually a morning.

    Diaphragm Pressure Tank Replacement in Baltimore, MD

    Diaphragm tanks use a flexible sheet clamped inside the shell. The diaphragm splits air from water. When it fails, symptoms match a bladder tank, with short cycling, pressure swings and water spitting from the air valve. Replacement is the usual answer.

    Bladder Pressure Tank Replacement in Baltimore

    Listen for the switch clicking every few seconds. That fast on-off pattern means the tank has no usable air, and the pump is now taking every hit of demand directly, so each glass of water starts the motor. We swap the tank and the cycling stops.

    Well-Rite Pressure Tank Installation in Baltimore, MD

    Well-Rite tanks use a replaceable diaphragm design in many models. We install them in the common residential sizes. Like any tank, the key is setting pre-charge before it fills, then confirming the gauge, switch and drawdown line up with the pump you have. A good tank installed badly fails early.

    Pressure Switch and Gauge Integration in Baltimore, MD

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Switch and tank are a team. A switch set at 40/60 needs a tank pre-charged to 38 psi, and a gauge that reads correctly so you can see what the system is doing, and a cheap new gauge beats guessing. Small parts, big role.
