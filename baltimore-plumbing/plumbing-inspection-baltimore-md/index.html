    Hard Water Damage Assessment in Baltimore

    Most municipal water across Maryland runs moderately hard, so scale slowly builds inside water heaters, faucet aerators and shower valves over the years. Hard water leaves scale in pipes, fixtures and water heaters. We look for crusty aerators, scaled showerheads, slow-filling toilets and sediment in the water heater, and we can run a simple hardness test on the spot. You get options, not a pitch. A softener, a filter or a scale-reducing system can extend heater and fixture life, and we tell you which one fits the hardness we measured.

    Whole-Home Plumbing Inspections in Baltimore

    Our whole-home inspections focus on the problems we see most in Maryland houses. That list starts with old supply pipe, worn angle stops, aging water heaters, slow drains and sewer lines with roots or sags. We test each one directly. Catching a corroded fitting or a partial sewer blockage now keeps it from becoming a ceiling stain or a backup on a holiday weekend.

    Commercial Property Plumbing Checks in Baltimore

    Businesses in Baltimore use our inspections to plan maintenance instead of reacting to breakdowns.     Water Heater and Softener Inspection in Baltimore

    We check the water heater's age by serial number, look for rust, leaks and sediment and test the relief valve. Venting and gas connections get checked. Expansion tanks, drain pans and softeners get a look as well, and a quick hardness test tells us whether the softener is actually doing its job or just using salt.

    Rental and Commercial Property Inspections in Baltimore

    Landlords and property managers use our inspections between tenants or before buying an investment property. We document each unit. We note heaters, fixtures and sewers by unit, and commercial jobs can include backflow and grease lines, giving owners a budget for repairs.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Plumbing Inspection in Baltimore

    Standard plumbing inspections usually cost $150 to $300. With the sewer camera, most totals land between $250 and $500.

    Watch for a water bill that jumps for no reason, low pressure, slow drains or brown water. Gurgling drains count too. A damp wall, a musty cabinet or a warm spot on the floor can mean a hidden leak, and an inspection finds it before it spreads.

    Watching your water meter, looking under sinks and checking for leaks are all things you can do yourself. A plumber adds video, pressure tests and an expert read.

    We identify supply pipe materials including polybutylene, Kitec, galvanized, copper, CPVC and PEX. We note known risks and replacement options.

    Most residential inspections in Baltimore take one to two hours. Age and size matter. House size, plumbing age and whether a leak or sewer problem needs extra tracing all affect how long the visit lasts.

    We inspect all the common brands and materials. Heaters from every major maker are covered. We work with PEX, copper, CPVC, PVC, cast iron, galvanized and poly, and we know the usual weak points for each and for common fixture brands.

    A standard home inspection takes about one and a half to three hours. Sewer video adds time. Larger homes, long sewer lines or a hidden leak that needs tracing can push it longer, and we will tell you the expected time when you book so you can plan around it.

    Every one to two years works for most homes. Some houses need it yearly. If your home has original supply pipe, large trees near the sewer or a history of leaks, a yearly inspection catches problems while they are still small and cheap to fix.
