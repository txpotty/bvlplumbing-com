    How We Repair Basement Leaks in Baltimore, MD

    New Sump Pump Installation in Baltimore

    Installing a new sump means breaking out a section of slab, setting a lined pit, and running the discharge out through the rim joist. Most of our installs get a cast iron pump, a check valve and 1-1/2 inch discharge. Discharge cannot go into the sewer in most places. It usually ends outside, well away from the foundation, at a spot your local rules allow.

    Sump Pump Installation & Repair in Baltimore

    A sump pump sits in a pit below the slab and kicks on when a float rises. Stuck floats, worn motors and failed check valves cause most of the service calls we run on sumps. We test yours with a bucket. Add a battery backup if storms knock out power.

    Outside Wall Waterproofing in Baltimore, MD

    Excavating the outside of a foundation puts the repair where the water actually gets in. Water heaters work hardest during the cold months, when incoming water temperatures drop and household demand for hot water climbs. We pick a membrane rated for that exposure, lay new perforated drain pipe at the footing in clean stone, and backfill so the grade slopes away from the house at least six inches over the first ten feet. Downspouts get extended too.

    Drain and Sewer Pipe Leak Repair in Baltimore, MD

    Cast iron drain stacks in older homes rust through from the inside over decades. A wet spot at the base of the stack or a crack along the hub is common, and we replace the damaged section with PVC or new cast iron joined with no-hub couplings. Plastic joints can fail too. Loose PVC joints usually trace back to poor support or a fitting that was never fully seated.

    Commercial Basement Waterproofing in Baltimore

    Commercial basements hold inventory, records or mechanical rooms that cannot sit in water. We look at floor drains, elevator pits and sump systems as one system, since a single failed check valve or a clogged pit inlet can flood the whole lower level. Duplex pumps help. Alarms on the pit tell staff before water reaches the floor.

    Crack Injection for Basements in Baltimore

    Injection works on poured concrete walls, not block. We set ports along the crack, seal the face with epoxy paste, and pump in polyurethane or epoxy from the bottom port up until resin shows at the next port. Epoxy adds strength. For a crack that is actively wet, polyurethane works better since it reacts with water.

    Floor Drain and Backwater Valve Repair in Baltimore

    A floor drain that backs up during heavy use or a storm usually points to a blockage downstream or a failed backwater valve. We cable or jet the line, then camera it to confirm. In homes where the street sewer backs up in storms, a backwater valve stops sewage from rising through the floor drain. Dry traps smell.

    Hydrostatic Pressure Relief in Baltimore

    High groundwater pushes up through the slab as well as in through the walls. You may see water coming up at the floor cracks or around the floor drain during a long wet week. Under-slab drain pipe tied to a sump lowers the water level beneath the floor. It is not a quick job.
