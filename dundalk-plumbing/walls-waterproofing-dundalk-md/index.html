    Interior Walls Waterproofing in Dundalk, MD

    Interior drain tile handles water that already got through the wall. We cut a channel in the slab along the perimeter, lay perforated pipe in washed stone, and tie it into a sump pit so the pump can push the water outside. The wall still gets wet. The difference is that the water no longer ends up on your floor. This route costs less than digging outside and works where a driveway or deck blocks the wall.

    Waterproofing for Older Homes in Dundalk, MD

    Houses built before modern drainage rules often have no footing drain at all, or a clay tile drain that collapsed decades ago. Homes built after the war usually have copper supply lines, while newer construction relies on PEX and plastic drain piping. Fieldstone and old brick foundations need breathable products. A rigid coating on soft brick traps moisture and makes the face spall off over a few seasons, so we use lime-compatible or vapor-open coatings and pair them with interior drain tile. Old mortar joints get repointed first.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Dundalk

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most reliable fix for a leaking basement, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing so water has somewhere to go. Basements are common in much of the state, while homes on the low coastal plain more often sit on crawlspaces or slabs because of a high water table. We backfill after the drain is tested.
