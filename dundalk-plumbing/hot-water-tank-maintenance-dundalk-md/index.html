    Relief Valve Testing in Dundalk

    Temperature and pressure relief valves on residential tanks are typically rated to open at 150 psi or 210 degrees, and they can stick shut after years of mineral buildup. Our tech lifts the lever, lets water flow, then watches it close. If it keeps dripping, we replace it.     Electric Thermostat Service in Dundalk

    Each element on an electric tank has its own thermostat, and the upper one carries a reset button for the high-limit switch. When the reset trips, something caused it. We trace the cause, often a shorted element or a stuck thermostat, before resetting it. Power stays off at the breaker the whole time.

    Expansion Tank Service in Dundalk, MD

    A thermal expansion tank is the small steel tank on the cold line above the heater, and it gives heated water somewhere to go when a check valve or pressure reducer closes the system. Our tech checks the air charge with a tire gauge. The precharge needs to match static pressure in the house. A waterlogged expansion tank sounds solid when you knock on it, and it lets pressure spike every time the burner fires.

    Electric Element Check in Dundalk

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. We test each one with a meter. If the hot water quits halfway through a shower, the lower element is the first suspect. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.

    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Dundalk, MD

    The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the steel tank does not. A water test is a smart first step around Dundalk before choosing a treatment system, because hardness and minerals vary from one supply to the next. We break the hex head loose, pull the rod and check how much bare wire shows. If six inches of wire is bare, it is time for a new one.

    Sediment Flush and Descaling in Dundalk

For stubborn scale we use a potable-rated descaler, then flush it clean. A clean bottom puts less heat stress on the seam.
