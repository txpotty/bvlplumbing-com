    Main Water Line Replacement in Dundalk

    Your service line runs from the meter or curb stop to the main shutoff inside the house, usually as 3/4-inch or 1-inch pipe. Once it has leaked in two different places, patching stops making sense. We dig at both ends, pull or cut out the old pipe, and run new line at the depth local code calls for, with fresh connections at the meter and at the shutoff. Most homes are done in a day.

    Copper Service Line Installation in Dundalk, MD

    Copper is still the choice for some homeowners. Plenty of copper mains have lasted 60 years. We flare or use brass compression fittings at the ends, keep the run in one piece between them, and pad it with sand to keep stones off. Where it goes through a concrete wall, it gets a plastic sleeve and a seal to keep water and bugs out.

    Type L vs. Type K Copper in Dundalk, MD

    The thickest wall belongs to Type K, the usual choice underground. Type L is thinner and mostly used inside the house. Some codes allow L for burial, but we stick with K for service lines because a thicker wall buys more years against stone contact and soil chemistry. Same fittings, different pipe.

    Main Water Line Repair vs. Replacement in Dundalk

    One leak on a twenty-year-old copper line? We will usually just fix that spot. Three leaks on a galvanized line from the 1950s is different, because the pipe is thin everywhere and the next leak is already forming, so replacing the whole run is cheaper over five years. We tell you which camp you are in and show you the pipe we cut out.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Dundalk, MD

    Bursting fits old cast iron or galvanized lines that will shatter under the head. Pulling suits softer pipe like poly or copper. Either way the new pipe follows the old path, which matters when the route runs under a patio or a neighbor's fence, and the yard work shrinks to two pits. Rock can stop it.

    Galvanized Main Line Replacement in Dundalk

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s. Inside, the pipe rusts once the zinc is gone. After a few decades the opening can be half its original size, which is why pressure drops and the water runs orange after a shutoff, and no amount of cleaning brings it back. It has to come out.
