    Why Polybutylene Fails in Aspen Hill

    Polybutylene reacts with oxidizers like chlorine that water utilities use to disinfect tap water. Over the years, the pipe and its acetal fittings get brittle from the inside, then crack along the inner wall until one splits under normal pressure. Most municipal water across Maryland runs moderately hard, so scale slowly builds inside water heaters, faucet aerators and showerheads over the years. Hot water speeds it up. Hot lines and fittings near the water heater fail more than anything else.

    Finding Polybutylene Leaks in Aspen Hill

    A poly leak inside a wall or ceiling can run for days before you see a stain. Watch the water meter. With every fixture off, a spinning leak indicator on the meter means water is going somewhere, and we use acoustic listening gear and thermal imaging to find it without opening walls at random. Once we find it, a temporary fix buys time to schedule the repipe.

    Polybutylene Identification and Inspection in Aspen Hill

    Polybutylene is usually gray, though it can be white, blue or black, and is often stamped PB2110. Start at the water heater. You may also see it under sinks, near the main shutoff or coming through the garage wall, and we trace runs through the attic to confirm how much of the house it serves.
