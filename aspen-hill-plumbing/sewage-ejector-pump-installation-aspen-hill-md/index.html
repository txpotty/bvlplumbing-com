    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Aspen Hill

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so the toilet, shower and sink drain to a sealed basin instead of flowing by gravity. We saw the floor, trench drains to the basin, set it, carry a two-inch vent to the roof and run discharge to the main drain through a check valve and shutoff. Then we patch the concrete.

    New Construction Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Aspen Hill, MD

    On a new build, we set the basin ahead of the slab pour. With no concrete to cut, it costs the least then, and we stub drains, vent and discharge to the plans for a clean rough-in inspection. It keeps the pour on schedule.

    Zoeller Ejector Pump Installation in Aspen Hill, MD

    Zoeller has made ejector pumps for a very long time, and its cast iron models are the ones we see still running in older basements. They work well for new systems and for drop-in replacements, since many Zoeller models fit existing pits and discharge piping as is. Floats and switches are stocked locally.

    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Aspen Hill

    Residential ejector systems in Aspen Hill serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. Each system needs a sealed basin, a pump with a float, a check valve and a vent. The parts are simple. Install mistakes cause more trouble than hardware, so the vent and lid seal get extra attention.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin Installation in Aspen Hill, MD

    The basin holds everything, and a 30-gallon size is common for a single basement bathroom. A larger basin means longer, less frequent cycles that spare the motor and switch, so we go bigger when the pit takes a laundry or several fixtures. Lids must be gas-tight.

    Commercial Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Aspen Hill, MD

    Businesses in Aspen Hill, MD with restrooms, kitchens or break rooms below grade need ejector systems that keep up with heavier, steadier use than a house. A duplex system with an alternating panel is common, so if one pump fails the other keeps things running. Downtime costs money. Each pump can carry the load alone.

    Emergency Ejector Pump Replacement in Aspen Hill, MD

    A dead ejector pump means the basement fixtures stop working until it is fixed. Quit using the basement fixtures and the washer if it feeds the pit, then call (443) 719-1650 and we will try to get a plumber and a replacement pump to you that day. Replacements are often quick.

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Aspen Hill

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps look alike in a catalog but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. Never mix the two.
