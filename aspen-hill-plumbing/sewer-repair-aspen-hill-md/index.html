    Orangeburg and Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Aspen Hill

    Orangeburg is wood fiber soaked in coal tar. It flattens with age. Cast iron from the same era rusts from the inside, builds scale and eventually splits along the bottom where the sewage runs, so by the time a camera shows widespread damage, spot repairs are a poor investment. We replace these lines with PVC. Trenchless bursting is often an option.

    Sewer Line Hydro-Jetting in Aspen Hill

    A hydro-jetter pushes water at several thousand psi through a nozzle that scours the full inside of the pipe. A cable only punches a hole. That makes jetting the better choice for grease, scale and fine root growth, and it is the standard prep step before any pipe lining job. Brittle or broken pipe is a different story. We camera first to make sure the pipe can take the pressure.

    Root Intrusion Removal in Aspen Hill, MD

    Roots enter through hairline cracks and loose joints, then grow into a mat that catches paper and grease. Cut roots regrow within a year or two. We clear the line with a root cutting head or a jetter with a chain flail, camera it afterward to see the condition of each joint, and tell you whether sealing or replacing those joints makes more sense than cleaning every spring. Mature trees in Aspen Hill yards make this a common call.

    Sewer Line Backups & Blockages in Aspen Hill

    Water coming up in the tub when the washer drains is a classic main line symptom. The clog is past the branches. Older Maryland homes with clay lines often show roots right at the joints. We record what we find.

    Sewer Repair Cost Comparison in Aspen Hill, MD

    A spot repair is the cheapest fix when the rest of the line is sound. Lining costs more per foot.     Root Intrusion Repair in Aspen Hill, MD

    Cutting roots treats the symptom. Fixing the joint treats the cause.     Sewer Pipe Relining in Aspen Hill, MD

    Relining builds a new pipe inside the old one. The old pipe gets jetted clean before anything else. A flexible liner saturated with epoxy is pulled or inverted into place, inflated against the old wall and left to cure, so every crack and leaking joint is covered by one jointless pipe. The finished pipe loses a little diameter. On a four-inch residential line, that rarely matters.
