    Keeping a New Sewer Line Healthy in Towson

    New pipe still needs a little care. Plant thirsty trees away from the line, since big roots can push on even a sound pipe. Clay that swells when wet and shrinks when dry can stress sewer laterals and water service lines over time. Flushable wipes still clog. On older homes, a quick camera look every few years spots trouble at the tap before it backs up.

    Upgrading Old Sewer Pipe Materials in Towson

    Clay, cast iron and Orangeburg all fail in their own ways. Roots get into clay joints, cast iron rusts out along the invert, and Orangeburg fiber pipe squashes flat over the years. PVC and HDPE do none of that. Fused HDPE has no joints at all, so roots have nowhere to start.

    Cured-in-Place Sewer Lining in Towson

    Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. We clean the line first, often with a cutter and a jetter, then pull a resin-saturated felt tube into place and inflate it so it cures hard against the host pipe. Collapsed pipe will not work. Lining shines under finished basements and slabs. We say plainly whether your line qualifies.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Towson

    A collapsed run or a long belly usually means we dig. We open a trench along the old line, pull the broken pipe and lay new PVC on compacted bedding at a steady fall of an eighth to a quarter inch per foot. Sod gets cut and rolled aside first when the lawn allows it. The inspector sees open pipe. We compact the backfill in layers, which keeps a dip from forming along the trench.
