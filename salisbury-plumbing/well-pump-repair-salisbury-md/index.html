    Variable speed drives like the Grundfos SQE and Franklin SubDrive keep pressure steady by changing motor speed. A fault code on the drive points us in the right direction.     Franklin Electric Well Pump Service in Salisbury, MD

    Franklin Electric motors and control boxes are in a lot of local wells. Our technicians know them well, from the QD control boxes to the SubDrive constant pressure units.     Jet Pump System Repair in Salisbury, MD

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Air leaks on the suction side cause most failures. BVL Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and watches it reach cut-out pressure. Nonstop running without pressure usually means lost prime or an air leak.

    Jet Pump Repair in Salisbury, MD

    Shallow wells and booster systems often use jet pumps. Common problems are loss of prime, a leaking shaft seal and a worn impeller. Our crew in Salisbury restores prime, replaces worn parts and checks the suction line for leaks that let air in. Where water levels swing through the year, a falling level can starve the pump and cost it prime.

    Control Box and Electrical Repair in Salisbury, MD

    Capacitors, relays and corroded terminals inside the control box cause many pump failures. A tripped breaker is often the first sign. Licensed technicians test these parts safely with the power locked out, replace what failed and tighten every connection in the box. Wiring meets Maryland code before we restart the pump.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Salisbury

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. The usual signs are no water at all, weak pressure or a hum with no flow. Testing starts at the surface. If it has to come up, a pump hoist lifts the pipe safely, and we replace the burned motor, worn impellers or a bad check valve before setting it back.

    Pressure Tank Problems in Salisbury

    Short cycling is the classic pressure tank symptom, with the pump clicking on and off every few seconds while a faucet runs. A waterlogged tank or a ruptured bladder causes most of it. Precharge gets checked on an empty tank. Set it 2 psi under cut-in, which means about 38 psi for a 40/60 switch.
