    What Goes Into a Grease Trap Install in Salisbury, MD

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Salisbury

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow. We pipe the air intake the way the manufacturer and the code both require. Get that wrong and the trap siphons. We also set the unit level within a quarter inch, since a tilted trap short-circuits the baffles and loses capacity.

    Zurn Grease Trap Installation in Salisbury

    Zurn's indoor grease interceptors cover the common sizes from small under-sink units up to 100 GPM, and we set enough of them to know where the gaskets give trouble. After a few years of hot water and scraping, the lid gasket is what usually leaks. We keep spares on the truck. Most Zurn units come with a flow control fitting, and we mount it on the inlet side at the height the instructions call for.

    Food Truck Commissary and Ghost Kitchen Traps in Salisbury, MD

    Commissary kitchens see a dozen trucks dumping wash water in the same two hours, which is a different load from a restaurant spreading it over a long shift. Sizing has to cover that short, heavy spike. Shared delivery-only kitchens can have more sinks per square foot than any dining room, and the trap must handle all of them. Each brand's prep sink counts.

    Grease Trap Sizing and Placement in Salisbury, MD

    Sizing starts with fixture drain rates and ends with where the trap can physically go. A trap 40 feet from the sink lets grease cool and stick in the pipe before it ever reaches the unit. Closer is better. For outdoor tanks we measure the hose run from where a pump truck can park.

    Commercial Kitchen Grease Trap Installs in Salisbury

    Every commercial install starts with a fixture list. We count each sink, floor drain and dish machine that will tie in, then add the gallons per minute each one can dump. From there we pick a hydromechanical unit or a gravity tank and figure out where it can sit with a straight shot to the building sewer. Fewer bends, fewer clogs.

    Gravity Grease Trap Installation in Salisbury

    A gravity interceptor works on time, not machinery. The tank holds water for roughly half an hour, which gives grease time to rise and food solids time to drop. We size by fixture count and retention time, then match the tank to what the local pretreatment office for your building will accept. No moving parts to fail.

    Grease Trap Brands We Install in Salisbury

    Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura cover most of what we set in commercial kitchens, plus precast concrete tanks for the big outdoor interceptors. The lid gaskets and baffles differ from brand to brand. Parts availability matters. We lean toward units with parts a local supply house keeps on the shelf, so a cracked lid does not close you for a week.

    Automatic Grease Removal Units in Salisbury

    An automatic grease removal unit heats the trapped grease and skims it into a container on a timer, so staff empty a bucket instead of scraping a tank. They cost more up front. Plan for a dedicated circuit near the unit, which your electrician will need to run. Fry-heavy kitchens like them most.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Salisbury

    Small under-sink hydromechanical traps usually land between $1,000 and $3,000 installed, and buried interceptors with excavation commonly run $5,000 to $20,000 or more. The spread comes from access and how much concrete or paving we cut. Permits add a little. You get a written, itemized number after we see the kitchen.

    Plan on a permit. Grease interceptors are regulated by both the plumbing code and the local sewer use ordinance, so two offices may want to see the plans. You get copies of every approval for your files. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.

    The common rule is to clean before grease and solids fill 25 percent of the trap's depth. For a small indoor trap in a busy kitchen, that can be weekly. Outdoor tanks go longer. A gravity interceptor typically gets pumped monthly to quarterly, and some sewer programs set the schedule for you.

    Watch the pump-out bills. Rising service frequency means the trap has been outgrown or the baffles have failed. Smell is another clue. Slow drains near the trap and failed inspections point to replacement too.

    Zurn and Watts make up a lot of our indoor installs, with Schier and Endura when a plastic body makes more sense. Outdoor interceptors are usually precast concrete, or polyethylene where a crane cannot reach. Any certified brand works. What we will not install is an uncertified import trap, because inspectors reject them and the parts disappear after a year.

    In everyday talk the two names get swapped. The trade usually calls the small indoor unit a trap and the big outdoor tank an interceptor. Codes vary on the wording. Some plumbing codes call both of them interceptors and separate them as hydromechanical and gravity, so we use whatever terms your permit office uses.

    Nearly every food business needs one. Codes require one wherever sinks or dish machines send greasy waste to the sewer. A few low-grease operations get waived, and the sewer authority makes that call. Ask before you sign the lease.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. We like the plastic bodies from Schier where steel would rust out. Brand matters less than sizing. We pick the rating first, then the brand that fits the space and budget.
