    Why Choose BVL Plumbing for Grease Trap Cleaning in Potomac, MD?

    A grease trap works by slowing the water down long enough for fat to float and food to sink. Past about 25 percent full, the separation stops working. The grease then slips past the outlet into the lateral, cools into a hard white crust, and a cable machine only bores a narrow hole through it.

    • Prevent Backups, Pumping on schedule keeps grease from slipping past the outlet and setting up in the line.
    • Extended Lifespan, Less hydrogen sulfide in the tank means slower corrosion of concrete and baffles.
    • Odor Control, A pumped trap stops the sour, rotten-egg smell that decaying grease gives off.

    Under-Sink Grease Trap Maintenance in Potomac, MD

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps are rated by flow, usually 10 to 50 gallons per minute, and they hold far less than people expect. In a busy kitchen, that means cleaning every week or two. We take the lid off, remove grease and solids, scrape the baffles, check the flow control fitting on the inlet, and replace the lid gasket if it is flattened or torn. Most odor from these units comes from a bad gasket.
