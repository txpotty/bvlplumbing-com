    How We Install Showers in Potomac, MD

    Digital Shower Systems in Potomac, MD

    Digital shower systems like U by Moen and Kohler DTV need a little more planning. These valves usually sit in a nearby closet or vanity and need low-voltage wiring to the controller and an outlet close by. The outlet is an electrician's job. We plumb the valve and outlets.

    Accessible and ADA Shower Installation in Potomac, MD

    Accessible showers start with blocking in the wall. We add solid wood blocking for grab bars while the studs are bare, set the valve within reach of a seat and install a handheld on a slide bar at a height that works seated or standing. No high curbs. We follow ADA guidelines when you ask.

    Tiled Walk-In Shower Installation in Potomac

    Frameless glass walk-ins are popular, and the plumbing behind them has to be dead on. Valve, head, handheld and drain go in first, then the pan is sloped a quarter inch per foot to the drain. Then we test. The valve gets a pressure test and the pan a 24-hour flood test before tile.

    Shower Stall Installation in Potomac

    A one-piece stall needs a level, fully supported base. Fiberglass and acrylic bases go down on a mortar bed so they cannot flex, and flex is the usual reason a base cracks early. The drain connects from below. Flow and temperature get tested after trim.

    Gym and Locker Room Showers in Potomac

    Gym showers run back to back from open to close. Commercial valves with metered or push-button controls keep run times in check, and the drains are sized for heavy use. Accessible stalls get handhelds on slide bars. Work is phased so part of the room stays open.

    Shower Valve and Trim Installation in Potomac, MD

    The valve sits behind the tile for decades. We install pressure-balancing, thermostatic and diverter valves, set to the depth your trim kit needs and plumbed with integral stops when the valve has them. Trim goes on after tile. Waiting keeps the trim clean during tiling.

    Shower Valve and Fixture Installation in Potomac, MD

    Pressure-balancing valves are the baseline for any new shower. Thermostatic valves hold a set temperature and add volume control, which works well with rain heads and handhelds. Both prevent scalding. The outlet count usually decides it.

    Tub-to-Shower Conversion in Potomac, MD

    Tub-to-shower conversions are one of our most common jobs. We pull the tub, move the drain from the end of the tub to where the shower pan needs it and raise the valve to shower height, usually around 48 inches off the floor. On a slab, we cut concrete. No more stepping over a tub wall.
