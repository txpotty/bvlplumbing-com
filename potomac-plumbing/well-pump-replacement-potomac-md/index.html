    What We Replace on Well Systems in Potomac, MD

    Sandy Wells and Submersible Pumps in Potomac

    Sand kills submersibles early. A well that pumps sand will wear impellers down in a few years, so we check for grit in the old pump and talk about setting the new one higher off the bottom or adding a sand separator. Where the pump hangs matters.

    What a Well Pump Job Costs in Potomac, MD

    A well pump job usually runs a few thousand dollars, and the pump itself is often less than half of that. The rest is labor, wire, pipe and fittings, plus the hoist time to get it all down the hole. You see every line item.

    Jet Pump Replacement in Potomac, MD

    Jet pumps sit above ground, in a well house or basement, and pull water up by suction from wells where the water level is within about 25 feet. Deep-well jets with a two-pipe setup can reach further, but they work harder. Lost prime is the classic complaint. Most of the time a bad foot valve or a suction leak is letting water drain back, and we check those before condemning the pump.

    Drop Pipe and Wire Replacement in Potomac

    Galvanized drop pipe from the 1970s and 1980s rusts from the inside, and a pinhole at 150 feet will bleed pressure every time the pump shuts off. Depending on depth and pump weight, it gets replaced with schedule 120 PVC or poly. The wire gets checked too. Cracked insulation and taped splices get cut out.

    Grundfos Well Pump Replacement in Potomac, MD

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have a built-in soft start and dry-run protection, and they run on two wires with no control box at the surface. They cost more up front. The SQE pairs with a CU 301 controller for constant pressure.

    Jet Pump Suction Line Repair in Potomac, MD

    Foot valves and suction lines cause most jet pump trouble, not the pump. Air sneaks in through a pinhole in the suction line, the pump loses prime and the seal burns up running dry. The suction line gets a pressure test. A well that is too deep for suction needs a submersible, and we will quote that instead.

    No-Water Calls in Potomac

    Pump trouble rarely gives notice. One morning the faucet coughs air and then nothing, and by then the motor has often been struggling for weeks. Before we pull the pump, we test the switch and control box.

    Submersible Well Pump Replacement in Potomac

    A submersible pump hangs on drop pipe inside a 4-inch or 6-inch casing, often 100 to 400 feet down, so pulling one is a two-person job with a pump hoist. Sand in the impellers and lightning surges are the usual culprits. We megger the motor before we pull it. The reading shows whether the fault is the motor or the wire.
