    Galvanized Main Line Replacement in Potomac

    Galvanized steel was common in homes built before the 1960s. Inside, the pipe rusts once the zinc is gone. After a few decades the opening can be half its original size, and that shows up as weak pressure and orange water, and no amount of cleaning brings it back. It has to come out.

    Polybutylene Water Line Replacement in Potomac, MD

    Polybutylene is the gray or blue plastic pipe installed from roughly the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. Chlorine in the water breaks it down from the inside. The failures tend to be sudden splits rather than slow drips, and some home insurers refuse to cover homes that still have it. Worth replacing.

    PEX Service Lines for Long Runs in Potomac

    We buy PEX in long coils. That makes it the easy pick when the meter sits out at the road and the house is set far back, since a single coil reaches all the way without a splice. Fewer joints underground means fewer places a leak can start, and it is the joints that usually fail first.

    PEX Water Line Replacement in Potomac

    PEX costs less than copper and goes in faster, which matters on a long run. It does not corrode or build scale. We use PEX-a or PEX rated for direct burial, use only fittings listed for burial, and lay tracer wire on top so a locator can find it later, since plastic pipe does not show up on a metal detector. Cheap insurance.

    Main Water Line Replacement in Potomac

    Your service line runs from the meter or curb stop to the main shutoff inside the house, usually as 3/4-inch or 1-inch pipe. Once it has leaked in two different places, patching stops making sense. We dig at both ends, pull or cut out the old pipe, and run new line at the depth local code calls for, with fresh connections at the meter and at the shutoff. Most homes are done in a day.

    Water Line Materials We Install in Potomac, MD

    Our trucks carry Type K copper, PEX-a and HDPE. Each has a place. Copper suits short runs and high pressure, PEX handles long runs with bends, and HDPE is the usual pick for trenchless pulls and bores, where we need a tough, fused pipe that can be dragged a long way. All of it is listed for drinking water, meeting NSF 61.

    Copper Service Line Installation in Potomac, MD

    Copper is still the choice for some homeowners. It has a long track record. We flare or use brass compression fittings at the ends, keep the run in one piece between them, and pack sand around it so rocks do not touch the pipe. Where it goes through a concrete wall, it gets a plastic sleeve and a seal to keep water and bugs out.

    Main Water Line Repair vs. Replacement in Potomac

    One leak on a twenty-year-old copper line? A repair coupling is often the right call. Three leaks on a galvanized line from the 1950s is different, because the pipe is thin everywhere and the next leak is already forming, so the full line costs less than a string of repairs. We tell you which camp you are in and show you the pipe we cut out.
