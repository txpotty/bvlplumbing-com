    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Germantown

    Small under-sink hydromechanical traps usually land between $1,000 and $3,000 installed, and buried interceptors with excavation commonly run $5,000 to $20,000 or more. The spread comes from access and how much concrete or paving we cut. Permits add a little. We quote in writing after a site visit, with the trap, the labor and the permit fees listed as separate lines.

    From first call to final inspection, most indoor jobs take one to two weeks, and most of that is waiting on the permit and the part. The install itself is often a single day. Outdoor jobs run longer. Gravity interceptors can take three to six weeks end to end because of plan review and tank delivery.

    That depends on the code your jurisdiction follows and the type of trap. Rules split, since hot water and detergent can carry grease past some indoor units. We check before we pipe it. Food waste disposers are a separate question, and most codes do not allow a disposer to discharge into a grease interceptor at all.

    As close to the grease-producing fixtures as the space allows, so grease does not cool and coat the pipe before it reaches the trap. Indoor units need room above for the lid to come off and for someone to kneel and clean. Never under a cook line. Outdoor tanks should sit where the hauler can reach them and nothing gets parked on the lids.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. Schier's plastic bodies do not rust, which helps in hot, wet kitchens. Brand matters less than sizing. A well-known trap installed too small will still fail inspection, so we pick the size first and the brand second.

    The common rule is to clean before grease and solids fill 25 percent of the trap's depth. For a small indoor trap in a busy kitchen, that can be weekly. Outdoor tanks go longer. A gravity interceptor typically gets pumped monthly to quarterly, and some sewer programs set the schedule for you.

    It keeps cooking grease out of the public sewer. Hot greasy water cools as it moves through the trap, the grease floats to the top, and cleaner water leaves through the outlet. Grease that reaches the mains hardens into blockages, and the sewer authority bills the kitchens it traces back to. Your own drain lines benefit too.

    We start with a fixture list and the drain rate of each fixture. Each sink's volume and drain time give a flow rate, and the dish machine's spec sheet gives its flow. That gives the GPM figure. Indoor units match the GPM, and outdoor tanks get sized by volume and retention.
