    Budget Tub Faucet Options in Germantown, MD

    Not every tub faucet needs a full valve. A cartridge plus a same-brand trim kit can refresh an old faucet for far less than a new valve. That option comes up first. Three-handle faucets are the exception.

    Standard Bathtub Faucet Replacement in Germantown

    Single-handle and three-handle tub faucets come out differently. A single-handle valve often takes new trim and a cartridge from the front, while a three-handle valve usually has to be cut out of the wall and replaced with a modern pressure-balance body. We check before quoting. Two-handle models fall in between.

    Dripping Tub Faucet Replacement in Germantown

    Drips at the spout come from inside the valve. On a single-handle faucet the cartridge seals have worn, and on a two- or three-handle faucet the stem washers and brass seats have worn, and either way the drip gets worse every month. New internals end the drip. Cracked bodies get a new valve.

    Tub Faucet Style Upgrades in Germantown

    Wall-mount tub fillers are a popular upgrade. A long-reach filler or a slide-bar handheld changes the tub, and both usually need a new valve or diverter. We plan the rough-in first. The trim goes on last.

    Delta Tub Faucet Replacement in Germantown, MD

    Delta tub valves are some of the easiest to update. Delta Monitor valves take new cartridges from the front, and the MultiChoice body accepts future trim swaps. Popular Delta cartridges are on the truck. Older Delta valves sometimes need full replacement.

    Tub Valves in Older Houses in Germantown, MD

    Cast iron tubs and plaster walls change the plan. Plaster cracks wider than drywall when you cut it, so we open plaster carefully with a multi-tool and often go in from an adjacent closet, and the heavy tub stays put while we work on the valve above it. Plaster patches take time. Access panels avoid it.

    Freestanding and Deck-Mount Tub Filler Installation in Germantown

    Freestanding tubs need a floor-mount filler. The supply lines come up through the floor under the filler, which means cutting the slab or working from a crawlspace or basement, and the filler needs a mounting bracket anchored to the subfloor. Deck-mount fillers sit on the rim. Tub-only fillers need a temperature limit.
