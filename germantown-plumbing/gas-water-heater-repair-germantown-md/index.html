    Tank Leaks and Anode Rods in Germantown

    Corrosion starts at the anode rod and works its way into the steel once the rod is spent. Most municipal water across Maryland runs moderately hard, so scale slowly builds inside water heaters, faucet aerators and showerheads over the years. The tech pulls the anode, checks what metal remains and swaps it if bare core wire shows. A fresh anode, a drained tank and a relief valve that seats properly can add years to a heater in a Germantown home that has not started leaking from the shell. Water from the shell seam is another story. That tank needs replacing.

    Thermostat and Gas Valve Repair in Germantown, MD

    Scalding water at the kitchen tap is a valve problem until proven otherwise. Some controls drift high as the sensor ages, and some dials get turned past hot. Our plumbers in Germantown, MD measure the outlet temperature at the nearest fixture, compare it to the dial and replace the gas control if it is overshooting, because water at 140 degrees can burn a child's skin in a few seconds. A setting near 120 degrees works for most households.

    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in Germantown, MD

    Lukewarm showers and a tank that never catches up often trace back to the gas control. We check the thermostat setting, the immersion sensor and the valve's outlet pressure with a manometer. On most residential tanks the thermostat lives inside the gas valve body, so a bad sensor or a stuck magnet usually means replacing the whole control rather than one small part. Plan on roughly an hour for the swap. Every joint on the new valve gets leak tested.

    Pilot Light and Ignition Service in Germantown

    No hot water and a dark pilot window usually point to the ignition side of the heater. The tech pulls the pilot assembly and checks the thermocouple tip, the orifice and the igniter wire. A thermocouple that tests weak on the millivolt meter will not hold the gas valve's magnet open, so the pilot drops out a minute after you relight it by the label. Most trucks carry the common pilot kits.

    Commercial Gas Water Heater Repair in Germantown, MD

    Restaurants and salons in Germantown, MD burn through hot water faster than any house, and a down heater stops the business. Commercial gas tanks often hold 75 to 100 gallons and fire at much higher BTU ratings. We work on those larger atmospheric and power vent units, including the electronic controls, the burner manifolds and the piping that ties two tanks together on one supply. Every hour down matters. We can often schedule the repair before opening or after close.

    Burner Tray and Venting Repair in Germantown

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. We pull the burner, clear the ports and look at the flame arrestor screen underneath. Then the vent gets checked from the draft hood to the roof, because a flue that is loose, sagging or capped by a bird nest can push carbon monoxide back into your Germantown home. We hold a smoke pencil at the draft hood to see which way the air moves.

    Venting and Draft Repair in Germantown, MD

    Atmospheric tanks rely on hot exhaust rising through a metal flue with an upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot. Sections that pull apart, rust through or sag let combustion gas spill into the room. We fix the vent. Each joint gets three screws, the connector gets proper clearance from wood and drywall, and we run the heater for several minutes with a smoke test at the draft hood to watch the exhaust go up. We do not leave with the heater spilling.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Germantown

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. A propane tank needs its own orifice and a valve set for roughly 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. Wrong parts get swapped for the right ones.
