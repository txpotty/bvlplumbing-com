    What Goes Into a PEX Install in Germantown, MD

    Remodel and Addition PEX Rough-In in Germantown

    Rough-in heights matter more than people think. If the shower valve sits two inches low or the vanity stubs miss the cabinet, the wall gets reopened after tile, and that costs time and money. We check heights with your contractor before drilling. Then we pressure test with the walls open.

    Full Home PEX Repiping in Germantown

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside out. Give it four or five decades and the inside is so packed with rust that a half-inch pipe barely flows, and the first draw in the morning runs brown. We replace every foot of it with PEX. You get real pressure back, and the discolored water goes away with the old pipe.

    Commercial PEX Pipe Solutions in Germantown, MD

    Apartment buildings, small offices and churches in Germantown, MD can benefit from PEX as much as houses can. With a manifold and shutoff for each unit, a leak in one apartment only takes that apartment offline, not the whole riser. Rated walls need firestopping. Every penetration through a fire-rated wall gets a listed firestop sleeve or sealant.

    PEX for Remodels & Additions in Germantown, MD

    Opening a wall for a kitchen remodel is the cheapest time to swap out the old pipe. With the studs bare, we can run PEX back to a manifold or to the nearest good trunk, set new stub-outs for the sink and dishwasher, and add a line for the fridge ice maker. An addition gets sized on its own. A new bath at the far end of the house may need a three-quarter inch feed to keep pressure up.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Germantown

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and the honest answer depends on the job. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Either one lasts for decades. For tight attics we lean toward PEX-A.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Germantown, MD

    Gray polybutylene went into millions of homes from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. Chlorinated water breaks the pipe and its plastic fittings down from within, which means it can look perfectly fine until a fitting suddenly fails. Insurers ask about it. Swapping it for PEX can help with insurance and with resale.

    Whole-Home PEX Repiping in Germantown

    Low pressure at every faucet, across the whole house, usually points at the pipe and not the fixtures. In older homes it is rust-choked galvanized or polybutylene with failing fittings, and swapping a faucet cartridge will not help. Repiping does fix it. We replace every supply line from the main valve to each angle stop and test the system before closing anything.

    Copper to PEX Conversions in Germantown

    Pinhole leaks in copper rarely show up alone. Aggressive water, fast flow and soil contact under slabs can pit copper from the inside, and the first few pinholes are typically followed by more. A water test is a smart first step around Germantown before choosing a softener, because hardness and minerals vary from one supply to the next. Converting to PEX stops the cycle. We use listed transition fittings anywhere PEX ties into copper that stays.
