    Sump Pump Maintenance in Rockville, MD: Pits, Floats and Valves

    Annual Sump Pump Check-Ups in Rockville

    Once a year is the minimum. Each yearly visit covers the motor, the impeller, the float and the discharge line. Then we fill the pit and time how long the pump takes to empty it, because a slow drawdown is the earliest sign of a worn impeller or a partly blocked line.

    Battery Backup System Testing in Rockville, MD

    Storms knock out power when the pit fills fastest. A backup pump exists for exactly that hour. Our plumber reads the battery date, cleans corrosion off the terminals and tests the charger, because a failed charger can leave the battery flat within a season.

    Submersible Sump Pump Maintenance in Rockville

    Submersible pumps sit at the bottom of the pit, which keeps them quiet but hides the problems. We lift the unit out to look at the seals and the cord. A cracked cord usually means a new pump. We also clean the intake screen and check the housing for corrosion, which shows up fast on pumps that sit in standing water for years.

    Battery Backup Sump Pump Maintenance in Rockville, MD

    Backup pumps sit in the pit next to the main pump and run off a 12-volt battery when the power drops or the primary fails. Our plumber load tests the battery instead of just reading its voltage. Then we unplug the main pump and let the backup empty the pit on its own. Alarms get tested too.

    Float Switch Repair and Adjustment in Rockville, MD

    Float trouble causes more no-start calls than anything else. A tethered float can tangle on the pump body or catch on the side of the basin. Vertical floats hang up once minerals coat the rod. In tight pits where nothing has room to swing, we can switch you to an electronic sensor switch with no moving float at all.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Check-Ups in Rockville, MD

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, so the wear shows up in the shaft, the bearings and the float rod. The float rod gets checked for bends, and the stops get checked for slipping. Bearings get a listen. Rattling or a high whine at startup usually means the motor is near the end.

    Discharge Line and Check Valve Service in Rockville

    The check valve sits on the discharge pipe a foot or so above the pump and keeps the water in the line from falling back into the pit. A failed valve sends that water back down for the pump to move again. Motors wear out faster. We test the valve, look for a weep hole between the pump and the valve, and check every joint up to the wall.

    Elevator Pit and Garage Pumps in Rockville

    Elevator pits, parking garages and mechanical rooms all collect water that has nowhere to go by gravity, so a pump has to lift it out. Elevator pit pumps often need oil-detecting controls so hydraulic fluid never gets pumped out with the water. We test duplex systems and high-water alarms on a set schedule. Logs go to the property manager.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Maintenance in Rockville

    Most Rockville, MD homeowners pay $100 to $250 for a standard maintenance visit, depending on the pump type and how long since it was last serviced. The price includes the inspection, a pit cleanout and the water test. Parts and repairs are quoted separately before we start.

    Routine maintenance in Rockville, MD rarely needs a permit. A full replacement, a new discharge route or new wiring may need one, depending on what the local building department requires. You will know before we begin if a permit applies.

    Seven to ten years is normal for a quality pump. A pump left dry for months can seize early, so testing during dry spells matters.

    The check valve stops water in the discharge pipe from dropping back into the pit after the pump shuts off. Without one, the pump ends up moving the same water again and again. All that extra cycling shortens motor life and adds to the power bill.

    Yes, our standard maintenance covers the battery backup if you have one. We load test the battery and confirm the charger is working.

    Most visits run 45 minutes to an hour. A same-day float or check valve replacement adds a few minutes.

    Without a working battery or water-powered backup, the pit keeps filling during an outage and eventually spills over onto the basement floor. In a hard storm, that takes under an hour. Water-powered backups need city water pressure, so they do not work on a private well.

    Watch for a pump that runs nonstop, hums without moving water, cycles every minute or two, or grinds. Rust, a stuck float or a musty smell around the pit are warning signs too.
