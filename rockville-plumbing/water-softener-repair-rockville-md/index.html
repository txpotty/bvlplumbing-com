    Resin Bed Problems in Rockville

    Fouled resin is the usual reason a softener regenerates on schedule but still sends hard water to every faucet in the house. Iron and sediment coat the beads and block the exchange. Depending on the test, the resin gets cleaned or replaced. New resin restores full capacity.

    Control Valve Rebuild in Rockville

    Service, backwash, brine and rinse are the four cycles the control valve directs every time the softener regenerates, usually in the middle of the night. Over the years, seals, spacers and pistons wear down. Common signs are leaks at the head or constant drain flow. The valve gets rebuilt with the kit made for it.

    Bypass and Fitting Leak Repair in Rockville, MD

    Bypass valves, the O-rings where the valve meets the tank, cracked fittings and the drain line are the usual leak points. A minor drip still wastes water and damages floors and cabinets. We replace O-rings or fittings and test under pressure. A cracked mineral tank usually means replacement.

    Commercial Water Softener Repair in Rockville, MD

    Laundromats and restaurants in Rockville, MD run through soft water fast, and a dishwasher or boiler starts scaling the moment the softener stops working. Downtime costs money. BVL Plumbing repairs commercial-grade softeners, from twin-tank setups to big valve heads. Work can be done after hours.

    Injector and Brine Line Cleaning in Rockville, MD

    The injector, sometimes called a venturi, creates the suction that draws brine into the resin tank. Sediment and salt residue clog its tiny ports. Once it clogs, the brine tank fills with water instead of emptying, and the softener regenerates with plain water that does nothing to the resin. The injector and screen get cleaned or replaced, and the brine line gets checked.

    Softener Repair vs Replacement Cost in Rockville, MD

    Most softener repairs cost $150 to $450, while a resin replacement often runs $400 to $900 depending on tank size. A new quality softener installed typically costs $1,500 to $3,500. With a good tank and valve, repair is the better value. If the valve is obsolete and parts are scarce, replacing the head or the whole unit may make more sense than chasing used parts online.
