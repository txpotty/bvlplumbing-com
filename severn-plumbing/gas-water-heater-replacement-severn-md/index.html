    Picking a Gas Water Heater for Your Severn, MD House

    Rheem Gas Water Heater Service in Severn, MD

    Rheem tanks and their Ruud twins share most parts, which helps when a gas valve fails a few years in. We install both. Setup follows the factory manual, including the draft test.

    High-Efficiency Gas Water Heater Upgrades in Severn, MD

    Condensing gas tanks run around 90 percent efficient or better and vent through PVC, while a standard atmospheric tank sits closer to 60 percent. They cost more and need a condensate drain and an outlet. Big households see payback sooner.

    A.O. Smith Gas Water Heater Service in Severn

    A.O. Smith makes tanks under its own name and also builds the State line, so parts overlap a lot between the two brands. Sizing and gas hookup are part of the job. Exhaust venting gets checked against their specs.

    Atmospheric Vent Upgrades in Severn, MD

    Flue pipes on older atmospheric heaters rust at the joints and lose their upward slope, and a loose connector can spill exhaust into the closet. We replace damaged sections and the draft hood if needed. Then we test draft with the burner on.

    Gas Water Heater Efficiency Ratings in Severn

    Efficiency ratings on tank heaters are listed as UEF, uniform energy factor, and a higher number means less gas per gallon heated. Basic tanks land near 0.60 UEF. High-efficiency condensing tanks reach 0.80 or more and can qualify for rebates.

    Power Vent Water Heater Replacement in Severn

    Blower motors and pressure switches are the weak points on power vent tanks. A second blower failure on an older tank is usually the point where a new heater makes more sense. A dedicated outlet gets confirmed first.

    Commercial Gas Water Heater Service in Severn, MD

    Higher BTU inputs on commercial units mean bigger gas lines and larger vent sizes, plus a permit and inspection in most jurisdictions. That is all part of the job. Downtime stays short.

    Thermal Expansion Tank Installation in Severn

    Closed systems are the reason expansion tanks exist. With a check or regulator on the main, expanding hot water has nowhere to go and the pressure spikes each heating cycle. Code commonly calls for an expansion tank. We precharge it to your house pressure.
