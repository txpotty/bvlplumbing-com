    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Columbia

    Bathroom count drives the cost more than square footage does. Basement homes can cost more when fixtures sit below the sewer and need an ejector pump. We price gas piping as its own line. Ask us for a number after we see your plans, since square-foot estimates miss too much to be useful.

    For supply, we use PEX-A or PEX-B in most homes, sometimes with a copper main. Drains are PVC or ABS. Some builders want cast iron in walls beside bedrooms, since it is far quieter than PVC when an upstairs toilet flushes.

    We do, mostly on custom homes. We work from your architect's plans and suggest changes that shorten runs or improve flow. Stacking baths, moving the water heater closer to the main bath and adding a recirculation line are the usual ones. Small moves save pipe.

    PEX-A is our default for water supply in Maryland homes. It handles heat well and resists the scale and pinhole problems copper can get with aggressive water. We run drains in Schedule 40 PVC. Joints under the slab are kept few, and every turn uses a long sweep so a cable can pass.

    We handle the plumbing permit work and inspections on our scope. Your builder usually pulls the main building permit. We book every plumbing inspection, meet the inspector, and clear corrections before the next trade needs the wall.

    Rough-in on a typical house takes one to two weeks of crew time, spread over the build. Underground runs a day or two before the pour. Top-out happens after framing and takes three to five days, then trim waits until cabinets and counters are in, which can be months later on a custom home. Trim is one or two days.

    PEX handles most water supply in new homes. It bends around corners and needs far fewer fittings inside walls than copper does. Drains and vents are usually Schedule 40 PVC or ABS, depending on what the local code allows and what the plans specify for the building. We still use copper near water heaters.

    Slow drains in a brand-new house are the first sign. Gurgling or a sewer smell usually means a vent or slope issue. A camera inspection through the cleanout will show a belly, an offset joint or debris left in the line, and it is worth doing before the builder's warranty period ends. Construction debris is common.
