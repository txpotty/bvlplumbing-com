    Salt-Based Water Softener Systems in Columbia, MD

    Salt-based softeners use ion exchange, swapping calcium and magnesium for sodium on a bed of resin beads. BVL Plumbing sets them up with the right resin and brine tanks. We program regeneration by metered water use so the system only recharges when the resin is actually spent, which saves a lot of salt. They work best on very hard water.

    Combination Softener and Carbon Filter Systems in Columbia, MD

    City water often carries chlorine or chloramine that affects taste and smell. A carbon filter placed ahead of the softener removes those chemicals and protects the resin, since chlorine slowly breaks resin beads down over the years. Taste improves fast. We install twin-tank setups or dual-stage units.

    Fleck Water Softener Installation in Columbia, MD

    Fleck valves, especially the 5600SXT and 7000SXT, are on a lot of the softeners we install. They are simple to program, easy to rebuild and parts are stocked almost everywhere, which matters when a seal fails ten years in. Settings match your test. We confirm the setup before leaving.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Softener Installation in Columbia

    Water softener installation in Columbia, MD usually runs $1,500 to $4,000. System type, brand, capacity and how much plumbing work the install needs all move the price within that range. New loops cost more. We quote after an on-site test.

    Soap scum on shower doors, dry skin, dull laundry and white scale on faucets are the classic signs. A quick hardness test at the sink tells us for sure. Testing is free.

    Ten to fifteen years is typical for a quality softener. Unit quality, how hard your water is and how well the system is maintained all change that, and resin can be replaced to extend it. Yearly checks help.

    Half a day covers most residential installs, usually three to six hours. The time depends on your existing plumbing, whether a loop is already in place and which type of system goes in. A loop saves hours.

    White, chalky scale on faucets and dishes is the most common sign. Dry skin after showering, dull laundry, weak lather and lower water pressure from pipe buildup all point the same way. A test will confirm it.

    Adding salt every 4 to 8 weeks is most of the work. Once a year a plumber should check for salt bridges, worn seals and injector clogs. Do not let the salt run out.

    Sized and installed correctly, a softener should not noticeably drop your pressure. If pressure falls after the install, it usually points to an undersized valve or a clogged pre-filter, which our plumbers can fix.
