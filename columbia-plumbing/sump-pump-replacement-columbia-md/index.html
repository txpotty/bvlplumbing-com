    Backup Pump Options in Columbia, MD

    Nor'easters, tropical storm remnants and summer downpours can overwhelm storm drains across Maryland and push water toward basements. Outages tend to hit right when the rain is heaviest and the pit is filling fastest. A backup takes over when the main pump quits or loses power. Where water pressure is strong, a water-powered backup runs off the supply line with no battery at all, protected by a backflow preventer. Wells cannot run them.

    Elevator and Garage Pit Pumps in Columbia

    Elevator pits and parking garage sumps often require oil-sensing controls that stop the pump from sending hydraulic fluid or motor oil into the storm drain. We replace those pumps and controls to meet code. Two alternating pumps on one controller mean one can fail without flooding the pit. Insurers sometimes ask for proof.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Columbia

    Basement bathrooms below the sewer line drain into a sealed, vented basin, and the ejector pump in that basin has to pass 2-inch solids. It is a different pump entirely. We replace the ejector and its discharge check valve, reseal the lid gasket, and confirm the vent ties in. Leaky lids let sewer gas into the basement.

    Cast Iron Submersible Pumps in Columbia

    Cast iron sheds motor heat into the water around it far better than a plastic housing does, which is a big part of why a Zoeller M53 or Liberty 250 outlasts a hardware-store pump. Most swaps take one to two hours. We also check that the float has room to travel without touching the basin wall.

    Sump Basin and Pit Replacement in Columbia, MD

    Old clay tile or 18-inch plastic basins crack and fill with silt, and a pump in a basin that small cycles every few minutes during a heavy rain. We break out the concrete, set a larger poly basin with a sealed lid, and pour the floor back. More volume in the pit means fewer starts per hour. Fewer starts mean the motor lasts longer.

    Choosing a Backup Battery in Columbia

    Battery choice decides how long the backup actually runs. Most backups use a sealed AGM or deep-cycle battery around 75 to 100 amp-hours, which covers several hours of on-and-off pumping. We ask how often your pump runs in a storm and size the battery to match. Plan on a new battery every three to five years.

    High-Capacity Sump Pump Upgrades in Columbia

    If your pump runs every 30 seconds during a storm and still loses ground, it is undersized for the water coming in. First we time the pump cycles and measure the pit refill rate. The rest is simple arithmetic. Often a 1/2 or 3/4 HP pump with more GPM at your lift, plus a 2-inch discharge line, solves it.

    Combination Sump Pump System Replacement in Columbia, MD

    Combination units put a primary AC pump and a DC backup on one discharge manifold, so you get two pumps and two floats in a single assembly. They are handy when the basin is too small for two pumps side by side. We replace the whole assembly or just the failed side.

    Sump Pump Plumbers Near You in Columbia, MD

    Speed counts on a sump call. When a pump fails mid-storm, a basement can pick up inches of water fast, which is why the nearest tech gets the call. Most arrive with the right pump.
