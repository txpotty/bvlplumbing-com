    Liberty Pumps Installation in Wheaton

    Liberty ejector pumps are a common pick on our jobs, with cast iron bodies and two-inch solids handling on most sewage models. Their packaged systems come with the basin, lid, pump and check valve matched together, which cuts down on field fitting and gives the inspector a clean, listed assembly to look at. A full-port valve gets added.

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Wheaton

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps seem similar at first but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. Never mix the two.

    Duplex and Commercial Ejector Systems in Wheaton, MD

    Commercial buildings, multi-unit properties and busy homes often need duplex systems with an alternating control panel. The panel switches the lead pump each cycle so wear is shared, and if the lead pump fails or the flow is heavy, the second pump starts automatically and the panel trips an alarm so someone knows to call for service. Redundancy matters here.

    Ejector Pump Alarms and Backup Options in Wheaton

    A high water alarm should be on any ejector basin, since a pump failure can lead to a sewage backup quickly. Audible alarms on their own power or Wi-Fi alarms that text you give warning while the pit is still filling, not after waste reaches the floor. Nor'easters, tropical storm remnants and summer downpours can overwhelm storm drains across Maryland and push water toward basements. Battery backup is an option.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin Installation in Wheaton, MD

    The basin is where the system lives, and a 30-gallon size is common for a single basement bathroom. A larger basin means longer, less frequent cycles that spare the motor and switch, so we go bigger when the pit takes a laundry or several fixtures. Lids must be gas-tight.

    Ejector Pump Replacement in Wheaton

    Ejector pumps tend to run 7 to 10 years, depending on how often they cycle and what gets flushed. Watch for constant running, a hum or grind, repeated alarms and odor at the pit, since any of them means trouble is close. Old switches fail first.

    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Wheaton

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so every fixture drains to a sealed pit instead of flowing by gravity. We saw the floor, trench drains to the basin, set it, carry a two-inch vent to the roof and run discharge to the main drain through a check valve and shutoff. The slab gets repoured.

    Upflush and Macerating Toilet Systems in Wheaton

    Macerating systems like Saniflo add a bath fixture or two without breaking the concrete floor. It sits behind the toilet, grinds waste and pumps it through a small line, often three-quarter or one inch, to a drain above, which suits a small half bath. Venting still applies.
