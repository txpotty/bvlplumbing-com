    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in Bel Air South, MD

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Repeated leaks on galvanized pipe call for a new line. Age matters too, since most service lines past 40 years old are living on borrowed time. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. You see the cut-out section yourself.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in Bel Air South

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. Straight runs are ideal. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, and a length of HDPE trails behind it in the same path. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    Trenchless Water Line Repair in Bel Air South

    Trenchless work makes sense when the line runs under a driveway, patio or a row of mature shrubs. We dig a pit at the meter and another at the house. The new line travels between them without a trench. A pneumatic boring tool or a cable puller drags HDPE or PEX through the soil, or through the old pipe if it is a straight run, usually in one working day from start to backfill.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Bel Air South

    High static pressure shortens the life of the service line and every fixture behind it. The usual code limit is 80 psi. We gauge the pressure at a hose bib, and if it reads high we replace the pressure reducing valve with a Watts or Zurn Wilkins unit. Old gate valves that will not close get replaced with full-port ball valves. That way you can actually shut the water off.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in Bel Air South, MD

    Galvanized steel service lines rust from the inside out. By the time one leaks, the rest of the pipe is close behind. We can clamp or replace a short section to get you through, and sometimes that buys a few good years. More often we recommend replacing the whole run from the meter to the house with copper or PEX. That fixes the pressure too.

    Main Water Line Repair in Bel Air South

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. When it fails you tend to see wet ground, a spinning meter and weak pressure at every tap. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. If it still moves, the leak is buried. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, so the crew opens one small pit rather than trenching the whole run.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Bel Air South

    A minor leak repair often starts around $500. Replacing the whole line is a bigger number. A meter-to-house replacement or a trenchless pull can reach $5,000 or more on a long run or a deep line, and the price moves with pipe type, depth and whether we trench or bore. We quote it in writing after the inspection.

    We work on copper, PEX, HDPE poly, PVC and old galvanized steel. We also run into gray polybutylene now and then. For new work in Bel Air South, Type K copper and burial-rated PEX are our usual picks, with HDPE for pulled lines. The trucks carry transition fittings for every one of them.

    Look for a wet or extra-green patch in the yard. A hiss at the meter is a giveaway. Low pressure at every fixture, a bill that keeps rising and rusty or cloudy water can all point to a damaged line. The surest check is your meter. A moving dial means water is escaping somewhere.

    You can tighten an exposed fitting at a hose bib. The buried line is a different job. They need the leak located, utility marking, careful excavation and burial-rated fittings. A mistake means a bigger leak and the same hole dug twice.

    Two to four hours for most spot repairs. Replacements usually take a day. Trenchless jobs are often a single day too, though digging the two pits and setting up the boring equipment takes a few hours on its own before any pipe moves. In most cases your water is back on before evening.

    Keep trees and shrubs with big roots well away from the line. Mark the pipe route somewhere. Disconnecting garden hoses and shutting off outdoor spigots before the first hard freeze prevents many burst-pipe calls each winter. A pressure gauge on a hose bib tells you if the regulator is failing, and anything over 80 psi is worth fixing before it starts wearing on the line. On galvanized lines, planning a replacement beats waiting for the break.

    In most places the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. The homeowner owns the line to the house. Some providers draw the line at the curb stop instead, so before quoting we check exactly where the leak is. If it turns out to be theirs, we tell you and help you report it.
