    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Glen Burnie

    Small under-sink hydromechanical traps usually land between $1,000 and $3,000 installed, and buried interceptors with excavation commonly run $5,000 to $20,000 or more. The spread comes from access and how much concrete or paving we cut. Permits add a little. You get a written, itemized number after we see the kitchen.

    The common rule is to clean before grease and solids fill 25 percent of the trap's depth. Busy kitchens with small indoor traps often hit that mark every week. Outdoor tanks go longer. Many gravity interceptors are pumped every 30 to 90 days, and your local FOG program may set a maximum interval no matter what the trap looks like.

    As close to the grease-producing fixtures as the space allows, so grease does not cool and coat the pipe before it reaches the trap. Leave room to open the lid and clean it without moving equipment. Never under a cook line. Outdoor tanks should sit where the hauler can reach them and nothing gets parked on the lids.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. Schier's plastic bodies do not rust, which helps in hot, wet kitchens. Brand matters less than sizing. We pick the rating first, then the brand that fits the space and budget.

    Plan on a permit. Grease interceptors are regulated by both the plumbing code and the local sewer use ordinance, so two offices may want to see the plans. You get copies of every approval for your files. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.

    Pump-outs every couple of weeks on a trap that used to go two months is the clearest sign you have outgrown it or the baffles have failed. So is a sour grease smell near the floor sinks. Rust shows up too. On steel traps we look for pinholes at the seams, and on any trap we check whether the outlet line downstream is coated with grease that should have been caught.

    Coverage comes from two places. Your trap carries a factory warranty that varies by brand and material. Our written labor warranty covers the joints we made and how we set the unit. Damage from skipped cleanings falls outside it.

    We start with a fixture list and the drain rate of each fixture. Each sink's volume and drain time give a flow rate, and the dish machine's spec sheet gives its flow. That gives the GPM figure. Indoor units match the GPM, and outdoor tanks get sized by volume and retention.
